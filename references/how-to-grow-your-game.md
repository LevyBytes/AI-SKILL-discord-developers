# How To Grow Your Game

> Discord Developers reference. Original prose; identifiers preserved verbatim.

(Figure: A banner showing players chatting about playing a game together) Shipping a game is difficult, and building a durable audience is often harder. New games launch every day, including titles backed by major marketing budgets, so discovery, sharing, and retention require deliberate work. Discord provides tools that can support that effort. **Why Discord?** Discord reports over 90 million daily active users* and says more than 90% of Discord users play video games*. Gaming communities already use Discord across desktop, console, and mobile, so developers can use those existing social connections to encourage deeper engagement, longer sessions, and organic game growth. Discord also reports that 66% of Discord users play games with friends weekly*. Players often share what they are playing with friends, so make it easy for your players to promote the game naturally. Discord PC players had a median play time 6 times longer when playing with at least one Discord friend compared with playing solo*. Shareable social experiences can keep a game relevant for longer, even when the game is not traditional multiplayer. Daily puzzle score sharing, roguelike daily runs, and leaderboards can all create community moments and friendly competition. Adding Discord to a game development strategy can help form community, expand reach, and keep players returning. **Discord Internal Data, 2025*

---

### Build a Community to Capture Your Players

 Community planning matters whether the game is new or already in beta. Discord is widely used for official game communities; Discord reports more than 9,000 official game communities with over 80 million members*. A player who joins a game's Discord community can build a connection to the game beyond play sessions. Community members may become invested in the game's success, offer useful feedback, and bring in additional players. The server also gives developers a direct place to talk with players, share updates and fixes, and learn what players value. An active community with visible developers gives players reasons to stay engaged between releases and helps the game maintain long-term relationships. **Discord Internal Data, 2025*

### Get Your Game Seen With Rich Presence

 Rich Presence is the feature that shows what a friend is playing in the Discord client, and it can help make a game more visible. The [Discord Social SDK](https://discord.com/developers/social-sdk) lets you shape how Rich Presence appears for players in Discord by adding text, images, links, buttons, and other details. You can also include invites that let players join the game directly from Discord. With Rich Presence, player profiles can market the game organically and create a direct path into social play. The easier it is for players to show what they are playing, the more easily the game can spread through their social graph.

### Keeping Your Players Engaged

 A Discord bot or companion app can keep the game present for players even outside active play time. Use it to bring pieces of the game world into Discord, announce events, share achievements and leaderboards, and run giveaways or promotions. That keeps the game visible in Discord and gives players prompts to return.

>  **“Apps weren’t just background tools; many existed as in-world systems or characters, quietly turning routine Discord behavior into part of the ARG itself.”**

 *[Case study from Sabotage Studio](https://discord.com/developer-case-studies/sabotage). The case study discusses how community work and bots helped bridge time between game updates.* Bots and companion apps can carry the game experience into Discord and create return loops for players. A guild-installable bot can operate in the game's community server, while user-installable bots can be used anywhere by players for broader sharing. A strong bot can help players naturally share the game and its community. When players share high scores, leaderboards, builds, or achievements through the bot, friends may come back to compete or buy the game so they can participate. This creates re-engagement and community-building effects that continue even when the team is not actively posting.

### Which Strategy Should You Start With?

 You do not have to adopt every strategy at the same time. Start with the approach that fits the game's current stage and audience, then expand from there.

### Pre-launch or early development:

 Start with "**[How Do I Create a Community For My Game?](https://docs.discord.com/developers/game-development/how-to-create-a-community-for-your-game)**". Give early testers and first players a place to gather. A development-stage community can produce useful feedback and form a core group of committed players who will support the launch.

### Existing game:

 Start with "**[How Can I Get My Game Seen?](https://docs.discord.com/developers/game-development/how-to-get-your-game-seen)**". Rich Presence and the Social SDK help players notice what friends are playing and can let them buy or join from Discord, supporting growth through friend groups.

### Live game with an existing community:

 Start with "**[How Do I Keep My Players Engaged?](https://docs.discord.com/developers/game-development/how-to-keep-your-players-engaged)**". Build a bot that connects the game with Discord. Identify the most useful game information for players to share; the bot can celebrate achievements, announce events, and encourage leaderboard competition. This reinforces the connection between the game and its community.
